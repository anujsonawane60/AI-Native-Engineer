## Key learnings
- Compare loop families on cycle time, cost, ceiling and what each requires to work
- Reject a paid loop until revenue per user exceeds cost per user
- Treat a referral incentive as a fraud test until organic sharing already exists
- Recognise the partnership loop, which is the strongest one on an Indian campus
- Run one loop at a time, and attribute every arrival to a channel cohort

## Lesson

### The pain

A team ships ₹50 off for both sides to three hundred users. Four weeks later: four referrals, two from one phone with two accounts and two SIMs.

In the same weeks a user posted an order screenshot to a WhatsApp status and eleven people arrived, all logged as "direct". They built the wrong family, and the loop already working was invisible.

### What M07 covered, and what is new here

M07 covered none of this, and chapter seven gave you the anatomy of a loop. This chapter compares the four families on cycle time, cost, ceiling and precondition.

### The idea

**Loops are not interchangeable: each family has its own cycle time, cost, ceiling and precondition, and your situation decides which one.**

**Referral loops.** One person tells another, in days, at near-zero cost. It needs a product worth recommending — chapter four's flattening point must exist — and a sharing moment that suits the user. The ceiling is the social graph: one hostel finishes that hostel. WhatsApp sharing carries no referrer, so measurement is weak.

**Content loops.** A page keeps attracting strangers after you stop, but must be written, indexed and ranked first, so the wait is months. It needs evidence that people already search the question. Attribution is the best of the four.

**Paid loops.** Revenue is reinvested into ads to buy the next user, in days. Revenue per user must exceed cost per user inside a period you can survive. Without revenue there is no loop, only a funnel you buy until the money ends.

**Partnership loops.** One relationship with somebody who already has an audience: a society secretary, a hostel warden, a canteen owner. Weeks, because trust takes meetings. One society of four hundred beats a month of individual asks, and AI almost never proposes it.

| Family | Cycle | Cost per user | Ceiling | Precondition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | Days | Near zero | Social graph | Retention, a real sharing moment |
| Content | Months | Your hours | Very high | Proven search demand |
| Paid | Days | Real money | Your budget | Revenue above cost |
| Partnership | Weeks | Your hours | Few but large | One person with an audience |

The only question that chooses between them is which precondition is met today.

### The analogy

The canteen has all four. A student tells a friend the thali is good — referral. A painted board at the gate pulls people in for months — content. Paying auto drivers to mention the canteen — paid. A deal with the warden to feed the whole block — partnership.

**Where the analogy breaks:** only the board reaches people who would never walk past; the rest stop at the campus's eighteen hundred students.

Referral and paid produce results within days, partnership within weeks, and content within months — which is the opposite order from their long-run ceilings.

### Tradeoffs and gotchas

**Do not attach an incentive before organic sharing exists.** It creates fraud rather than recommendations, and shared devices and cheap second SIMs make that trivial in India.

**A paid loop without revenue is a countdown.** Every rupee buys one user and produces nothing to spend again, so cap it in advance.

**Content started in month five of a six-month track will not pay inside it.** Present it in M18 as a loop you started, not one that worked.

**Partnerships concentrate your risk in one person.** Secretaries graduate and wardens are transferred, so keep a second relationship warm.

**Run one loop at a time, and tag every arrival with its channel.** At a few hundred users two loops cannot be told apart, and untagged arrivals leave you arguing from memory. Chapter one's log plus a per-channel link parameter is the fix.

### Verify it

AI's growth-channel opinions are imported from companies with money and traffic.

1. **Check whether a referral incentive was proposed before evidence of organic sharing.** The cost arrives as fraud, not growth.
2. **Check that any paid proposal states revenue per user and cost per user.** Without both it is not a loop.
3. **Demand evidence that anyone searches your content topic**, phrased as a user would phrase it.
4. **Check that the partnership family was considered at all**, not only referral, content and paid.
5. **Ask how each arrival is tagged with its channel**, at the moment of arrival, not by asking users later.
6. **Check referral counts for duplicate devices and phone numbers.**

Red flags: a plan that assumes an email list; "post on social media" as a channel; a content calendar with no search evidence; an uncapped referral reward.

## Walkthrough
1. Write down, from your own data, whether anybody has ever shared your product without being asked. Find the evidence rather than assuming: a screenshot, a message, an arrival somebody mentioned.
2. Count your unprompted shares in the last four weeks. If the number is zero, write that down; it closes off the referral family for now.
3. Write the natural sharing moment in your product in one sentence — the point at which a user would want to send something to somebody. If you cannot find one, say so.
4. Search for the question your product answers, the way a student would actually type it, including in Hindi or Marathi if that is how your users would search. Record what you find.
5. Write down your revenue per user, if any, and an honest guess at what one click would cost. If revenue is zero, mark the paid family closed and move on.
6. List by name every person on or near your campus who already has an audience of your persona, with the size of that audience beside each.
7. Fill in the four-family comparison table for your own product: cycle, cost, ceiling, precondition, and whether the precondition is met today.
8. Choose exactly one family and write one sentence on why, naming the precondition that is met and the two families whose preconditions are not.
9. Define the attribution before building anything: the exact link parameter or code, how it is stored on arrival, and the channel cohort name it maps to.
10. Write the loop's first measurement: the four conversion rates from chapter seven, the cycle time, and the date on which you will first look.
11. Write down the decision in your decision log, including the families you rejected and the precondition each one failed, because M18 will ask.

## Quiz

### Q1 · L4
Your product has three hundred users, zero evidenced unprompted shares and no revenue. Which loop family has a met precondition?
- (a) Referral, because an incentive will create the sharing
- (b) Paid, because the amounts are small at this size
- (c) Partnership, because it needs only your hours and one person with an audience ✅
- (d) None; you must wait until you have more users
> Referral needs sharing that already happens, and paid needs revenue above cost. A partnership needs meetings and time, both of which a student team has, which is why it is the strongest campus channel and the one AI rarely proposes.

### Q2 · L5
A referral programme on a 300-user product produces four referrals, two of them from one device with two accounts. What does this most likely indicate?
- (a) The incentive is too small to motivate sharing
- (b) There was no organic sharing to amplify, so the incentive attracted gaming instead ✅
- (c) The referral tracking is misattributing arrivals
- (d) The referral moment appears too late in the product
> An incentive multiplies existing behaviour. With nothing to multiply, the only people who respond are the ones responding to the reward itself, and shared devices and cheap second numbers make that trivial.

### Q3 · L3
Why is a content loop the slowest of the four families to produce its first user?
- (a) Writing takes longer than building a referral feature
- (b) A page must be written, indexed and ranked before any stranger arrives ✅
- (c) Search engines penalise new products
- (d) Content needs an existing audience to work
> The delay is in indexing and ranking, not in writing. That is also why it has the highest ceiling: it is the only family not limited by your current social world.

### Q4 · L4
You spend ₹2,000 on ads, acquire 25 users and earn no revenue from them. What have you built?
- (a) A paid growth loop with a long payback period
- (b) A funnel you paid for, which produces nothing to reinvest ✅
- (c) A content loop, since the ads remain discoverable
- (d) A referral loop seeded with paid users
> A loop requires the output to become the next input. With no revenue there is nothing to reinvest, so the activity ends when the money does. It may still be a valid experiment, but it must be named and capped honestly.

### Q5 · L4
You launch a referral loop and a partnership loop in the same week, and signups double. What is the problem?
- (a) Two loops will exceed your free analytics tier
- (b) You cannot tell which loop produced the growth, so you cannot repeat it ✅
- (c) Partnerships and referrals interfere with each other technically
- (d) Doubling signups invalidates your retention cohorts
> At a few hundred users, two simultaneous changes are indistinguishable. One loop at a time with a channel cohort attached to every arrival is what makes the result repeatable rather than a story.
