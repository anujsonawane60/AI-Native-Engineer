## Key learnings
- Write a VOICE.md that constrains tone with example pairs and bans, never with adjectives
- Decide one language policy across English, Hindi or Marathi, and Hinglish — and hold it
- Keep one visual kit so three people's posts read as one company
- Reuse the same decision set on every channel instead of re-deciding each week
- Spot a caption set or image set that has drifted off brand

## Lesson

### The pain

Three teammates run the accounts for three weeks, then scroll the feed together in order. Post 1: "Dear users, we are pleased to announce the launch of our new feature." Post 2: "guys this update is sooo good 🔥🔥 go check it out ✨". Post 3: "NEW UPDATE AA GAYA! Abhi try karo!!" Below the words it is worse: four accent colours, one a violet nobody chose because it came with a template, two fonts, three crops of the logo, and the price written as `Rs. 1800`, `₹1,800/-`, `1800 rupees` and `₹1800 per month` in one week. Then a mentor asks "what is the product called?" and gets two answers from two founders. The team has not been building a brand; they have been building three.

### The idea

**A brand is a set of decisions, written down once, so that three people and one language model all make the same choice without asking.**

M03 chapter 7 gave you `DESIGN.md` for the product's surface. This chapter's deliverable is its sibling, **`VOICE.md`**, covering how you sound and look everywhere outside the app. Both exist because a decision living only in someone's head gets re-made differently every time, and a model that starts fresh each session takes the average unless you hand it the rules. Eight sections, two screens — a twenty-page brand bible is a file nobody opens.

| VOICE.md section | The decision it records | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Three voice traits | Each one a rule plus a do/don't pair | "Direct — the price in sentence one" |
| Who we talk to | The single word for your reader | "Parents", never "users" |
| Language policy | Which language, which script, when | Marathi captions, English site |
| Naming and spelling | Exact product and feature names | Capitalised one way, forever |
| Numbers and money | One format for price, time, date | `₹1,800`, `8:40 pm`, lakh not million |
| Reading level and length | Ceilings per sentence and channel | Second-year student, second language |
| Claims policy | What you may and may not assert | Ban "fastest in Nashik" in writing |
| Eight bans | Prohibitions checkable by reading a draft | No emoji in headings, no ALL CAPS |

Two rows carry most of the weight. Adjectives alone do nothing — "friendly" is the centre of the distribution, exactly like "modern" in M03 — so a trait is written as a rule with a pair: *Direct — we say the price in the first sentence. Do: "₹1,800 a month, delivered by 8 pm." Don't: "We're excited to share our affordable pricing!"* And the language policy names the one person who reads every non-English piece aloud before it ships, because machine translation produces grammatically correct sentences no real speaker would say.

The visual half is more mechanical. Decide once: one accent colour from `DESIGN.md`, one typeface, one logo lockup, one screenshot treatment in a device frame your users actually hold. Then build one template per channel ratio — square and vertical for Instagram, vertical for Reels, wide for a link preview, fixed sizes for M12's store screenshots — with text inside the safe area away from platform controls.

### The analogy

The canteen's handwritten price board: same hand, same two marker colours, same layout for ten years — thali left, snacks right, prices in the same squared-off numerals. You would recognise it from a photograph with the name cropped out. Nobody wrote a brand guide; one person wrote the board.

**Where the analogy breaks**, and it is the whole reason this chapter exists: that consistency came free because one hand did it. You have three hands and an AI collaborator whose memory resets every conversation, so everything that hand did automatically has to be externalised into written rules — otherwise your board is re-designed weekly by whoever holds the marker.

### Picture it

```
          ┌──────────────────────────────┐
          │ VOICE.md  +  DESIGN.md       │
          │ ONE decision set             │
          │  3 traits, each with a rule  │
          │  language policy             │
          │  naming + money format       │
          │  8 checkable bans            │
          │  1 accent, 1 font, 1 crop    │
          └────┬─────────┬─────────┬─────┘
               ▼         ▼         ▼
            website   social    store
             copy     posts    listing
     same name, same price format, same accent colour
```

One written decision set feeds every surface, so the website, the social posts and the app-store listing agree on your name, your price format and your single accent colour without anyone having to remember.

### Where it lives

`DESIGN.md` covers the product's interface, `VOICE.md` covers everything else, and the two must agree on colour and type. Downstream it feeds nearly everything: chapter 5's briefs paste the bans, chapter 7 generates imagery against the visual kit, chapter 8's scheduler adapts per channel while the voice stays fixed, and chapter 9's repurposing is only safe because the voice does not change between formats.

### Words to own

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Brand voice | How you always sound | Direct, plain, price-first |
| Tone | How you sound in this situation | Sober during an outage |
| VOICE.md | The written decision set for words | Two screens, eight sections |
| Example pair | A do and a don't side by side | Shown under each trait |
| Language policy | Which language, script and when | Marathi captions, English site |
| Code-switching | Mixing two languages naturally | "Abhi try karo" |
| Lockup | The fixed arrangement of your logo | Mark left, name right |
| Safe area | Margin platform controls may cover | Keep text 10% from edges |
| Aspect ratio | Shape of the frame | 1:1, 4:5, 9:16 |
| Template kit | Pre-made files per ratio | One per channel |
| Drift | Slow loss of consistency | Four accent colours by week 3 |
| Claims policy | What you are allowed to assert | No "fastest in Nashik" |

### Tradeoffs and gotchas

**Voice is constant; tone moves.** The same voice sounds different announcing a feature and apologising for four hours of downtime. A voice document that cannot express a sober apology is a costume.

**Consistency is not monotony.** Rules on format, naming, money and bans; freedom inside them on what you actually say. A file that dictates sentence structure makes you sound like a form.

**A regional language is a voice, not a translation.** Your Marathi presence needs its own example pairs, written by someone who speaks it. Translating your English voice word for word produces something stiff and faintly wrong.

**Beware the template's defaults.** The violet nobody chose arrived with a free template, exactly as M03 chapter 8 described. Open every new template and replace its colours before using it once.

**Review the file after six weeks of real posting.** The first version is a guess; the second is informed by what you found yourself writing.

### Verify it

**Red flag: a voice document made only of adjectives.** Every line a word like "friendly" and not one do/don't pair means nothing in it is checkable, and AI will ignore it.

**Red flag: the grid test fails.** Screenshot your last nine posts as a three-by-three grid and cover the account name. Could a stranger tell these are one company? Count the distinct accent colours while you are there: more than one means the kit is being ignored.

**Red flag: more than one way to write the price.** Search your own channels for your price and count the formats. Four formats means the file does not exist or nobody reads it.

**Red flag: AI "applying your voice" by adding enthusiasm.** Give it a paragraph and your `VOICE.md`. If the only change is more exclamation marks and a friendlier opener, it read your adjectives and skipped your rules. Ask it to cite which rule caused each change.

**Red flag: a non-English post nobody read aloud.** If the person named in your language policy did not read it, it did not pass the gate.

**Red flag: a claim you cannot support.** Search your copy for superlatives — fastest, best, cheapest, only — and check each against evidence you could show a user.

## Walkthrough
1. Collect your last fifteen published items — posts, captions, website sections, emails — into one document in the order they went out. Read them in sequence.
2. Mark every inconsistency you find: different product names, different price formats, different tones, different ways of addressing the reader. Count them.
3. Make the nine-post grid, cover the account name, and ask two people outside your team whether it looks like one company. Write down their exact answer.
4. Count the distinct accent colours, typefaces, logo crops and screenshot frames across those fifteen items. Each count above one is a decision you have not made.
5. Pick your three voice traits. For each, write one rule and one do/don't example pair taken from your own real copy, not invented.
6. Write the language policy, including which person reads every non-English piece aloud before publish. Name them.
7. Fix the mechanical sections: product name, feature names, money format, time format, date format, what you call your reader.
8. Write eight bans, each checkable by reading a draft, and at least one covering claims you cannot support.
9. Assemble it as `VOICE.md`, under two screens, and paste it beside `DESIGN.md` into a fresh AI session. Ask it to rewrite one old post to the rules and to cite which rule caused each change.
10. Rebuild one template per channel ratio with your accent colour and typeface, with text inside the safe area, and replace any colour that came from the template's defaults.

## Talk to AI
```prompt
CONTEXT:      I am a student founder in a team of three. We publish on
              [channels] for [audience: who, where, which languages].
              My product's exact name is [name], and we currently also
              call it [the wrong variants we use].

              Fifteen things we actually published, in order, pasted raw
              so you can see our real range: [paste all fifteen]

              My existing design tokens from DESIGN.md: [paste them]
              My accent colour: [hex]. My typeface: [name].

GOAL:         A VOICE.md under two screens long that three people and an
              AI can all follow without asking each other.

REQUIREMENTS: 1. First, audit my fifteen items: list every inconsistency
                 you find, grouped as voice, naming, money and format,
                 and quote the actual lines
              2. Propose exactly three voice traits. Each one must come
                 with a rule and a do/don't pair taken from MY pasted
                 copy, not invented examples.
              3. A language policy covering which language goes on which
                 channel, which script, and when code-switching is
                 allowed
              4. Mechanical rules: product name, feature names, money
                 format, time format, date format, what we call the
                 reader
              5. Exactly eight bans, each one checkable by reading a
                 draft, at least one covering claims we cannot prove
              6. A visual section: one accent, one typeface, one logo
                 rule, one screenshot treatment, and the frame ratios
                 per channel

CONSTRAINTS:  Free tools only. Must stay under two screens. Must agree
              with my pasted design tokens - do not introduce a new
              colour or font. Our audience reads in [languages] and
              many are on budget Android phones.

NON-GOALS:    Do not write a brand story, mission statement or tagline.
              Do not propose a rebrand. Do not use the words friendly,
              approachable, authentic or trustworthy as traits - they
              describe every brand and constrain nothing.

ACCEPTANCE:   - Every trait has a do/don't pair quoted from my own copy
              - Every ban could be checked by one person reading a draft
              - Zero new colours or fonts introduced
              - A section titled "Inconsistencies I found", with line
                quotes and counts
              - A section titled "What this file does not decide", so we
                know where judgement is still ours

ASK:          Before proposing anything, tell me which of my fifteen
              items sounds least like the others, and what it suggests
              about the voice we were reaching for. Then write the file.
```

## Stuck? Ask AI
```prompt
Here are three voice adjectives we picked: [paste them]. For each one, tell me honestly whether it constrains anything or whether it would describe almost any brand. Then, for each, ask me for one real sentence from my own copy that shows it, and turn that into a do/don't pair. Do not write the document yet.
```

```prompt
Here are nine of my recent posts described in detail — colours, fonts, logo placement, image style, caption tone: [describe all nine]. Cover my brand name mentally. Tell me whether these look like one company or several, and for every element that drifts, say which post is the outlier. Do not redesign anything, just diagnose.
```

```prompt
Explain the difference between brand voice and tone as if I have never worked in marketing, using one product announcing a feature and then apologising for a four-hour outage. Then give me four situations and ask me to say what stays the same and what changes, before you tell me the answer.
```

## Quiz

### Q1 · L3
Why is "friendly, approachable, trustworthy" a weak set of voice traits?
- (a) The words are too long for a style guide
- (b) They describe nearly every brand, so they constrain nothing and give AI no instruction ✅
- (c) Three traits is too few to define a voice
- (d) They conflict with each other in practice
> This is M03 chapter 8's lesson in words: adjectives at the centre of the distribution carry no information. A do/don't example pair from your own copy is the part that actually directs output.

### Q2 · L2
What belongs in the "numbers and money" section of VOICE.md?
- (a) Your pricing strategy and discount policy
- (b) One fixed way to write prices, times and dates — for example `₹1,800` and `8:40 pm` ✅
- (c) Your revenue targets for the term
- (d) The maximum discount a teammate may offer
> It is a formatting decision, not a business one. It is also the most boring section and the one that removes the most visible inconsistency across channels.

### Q3 · L4
You give AI your VOICE.md and a paragraph to rewrite. It returns the same text with more exclamation marks and a warmer opening. What happened?
- (a) The rewrite is correct; warmth is what voice means
- (b) It responded to your adjectives and ignored your rules, which means the rules are not checkable ✅
- (c) The paragraph was already on-voice
- (d) VOICE.md should be given after the paragraph, not before
> Enthusiasm is the default interpretation of any tone instruction. Ask it to cite which specific rule caused each change — unciteable changes mean your file is decoration.

### Q4 · L3
Your team publishes a Marathi caption by machine-translating the English one and posting it unread. What is the risk?
- (a) None; translation quality is now reliable
- (b) It will be grammatical but phrased in a way no Marathi speaker would use, which reads as careless ✅
- (c) The post will not be indexed by search engines
- (d) Marathi captions reduce reach on every platform
> A regional language is a voice with its own example pairs, not a translation of yours. This is why the language policy names a specific person who reads every non-English piece aloud before it ships.

### Q5 · L5
Nine of your recent posts, viewed as a grid with the account name covered, could belong to four different companies. What is the single most useful first action?
- (a) Hire a designer to rebrand
- (b) Count the distinct accent colours, fonts, logo crops and price formats, then decide each one once and write it down ✅
- (c) Delete the inconsistent posts
- (d) Post more often so the feed settles into a style
> Drift is a symptom of undecided questions. Every count above one is a decision nobody made, and writing each one down in VOICE.md is what stops it being re-made weekly by whoever is posting.
